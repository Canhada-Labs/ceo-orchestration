P1 — RecursionError in the new indirect guard fails OPEN through main()'s catch-all
FILES:
- .claude/hooks/_lib/bash_indirect_guard.py:386-399 (the xargs and find -exec branches of _scan_segment call it again with depth+1, but _scan_segment, def at :347, never checks depth; only _scan checks MAX_DEPTH, at :472)
- .claude/hooks/_lib/_bash_safety_parts/posture_scan.py:578-580 (_indirect_guard.scan is called outside any try)
- .claude/hooks/_lib/_bash_safety_parts/decisions_cli.py:243-249 (except Exception -> _contract.allow())
FAILURE SCENARIO:
- Command: "xargs xargs ... xargs rm -rf ~", with about 995 "xargs" words. That is about 1,000 words in roughly 6 KB, under MAX_WORDS=1024 (:21, enforced at :126) and MAX_BODY_BYTES=16384.
- No earlier check fires: _normalize_command_tokens does not strip xargs, so _check_rm_rf sees tokens[0]=='xargs'; E3 and E4 only look for canonical or toggle paths.
- _scan then hands the whole segment to _scan_segment, which calls itself once per xargs word. That passes Python's default recursion limit of 1000 and raises RecursionError, which is not a ValueError.
- The error goes through scan(), then decide_command(), then main()'s catch-all, which prints FATAL and emits allow.
- The "find . -exec find . -exec ... rm -rf / ;" form does the same.
- This is the wrong fail direction for input the matcher cannot handle. ADR-201:64-71 promises explicit depth limits and fail-closed handling of malformed bodies. The repo itself states this rule at posture_parser.py:367-370: "a RecursionError inside a security matcher is a crash — i.e. a fail-OPEN — not a deny".
- It is not a regression from v1.4.3-rc.1: legacy matchers never blocked xargs rm -rf. Status is derived from reading, not reproduced.
MINIMAL FIX: at the top of _scan_segment, add "if depth > MAX_DEPTH: return _hit('bash_indirect_limit')". For defence in depth, in decide_command catch RecursionError around _indirect_guard.scan and return Decision(allow=False, reason=_hit('bash_indirect_limit').reason, destructive=False, reason_code='bash_indirect_limit'). ImportError and other infrastructure faults stay fail-open.
