NEW FINDINGS (annex)
P1 — .claude/hooks/_lib/agent_spawn/file_assignment.py:327-379 (_recent_file_assignments reads only the canonical log, :340), together with _spool_writer_parts/lifecycle.py:346-350 and spool_writer.py:528-530.
Failure scenario: under CEO_HOOK_BOUNDED_EXIT=1 every audit event goes to the per-PID spool (_audit_emit_parts/writer.py:579-592), and the hook exits without draining.
- An opportunistic drain fires only when a process's own spool is more than 100 ms old at its next emit. Each drain moves at most the K_MAX=100 oldest entries.
- So check_agent_spawn (pid A) records spawn_file_assignment_recorded for "CAN edit: x.py" in state/audit-spool.A.jsonl, and the record stays there.
- The next sequential spawn (pid B) with the same path tails audit-log.jsonl only. It finds no reservation, so CEO_SPAWN_OVERLAP_GUARD=1 does not block the clobber.
- Before this train, pid A's atexit drain_now(force=True) appended the record before exiting.
- The guard is opt-in, so this fails open, but silently. Condition 8 and the Known-open declare pending spool work and unproven later collection. They do not declare that in-session readers of the canonical log go blind, including this governance rail and outcome_telemetry._tail_recent_rendered.
Minimal fix: have _recent_file_assignments also scan state/audit-spool.*.jsonl and *.draining.* body lines (same event JSON, same window filter). Alternatively, write spawn_file_assignment_recorded straight to the canonical log, bypassing the spool. Then declare the lag for other canonical-log readers.
